# Ball and robot dynamics fitted from SSL game logs

Generated by `ssl-logtool dynamics <logs> --out docs/calibration/dynamics.json`
(`crates/ssl-sim-logtool/src/dynamics.rs` + `src/dynamics/*`). All numbers are
SI, robust statistics (median, MAD-σ, standard error of the median
`1.2533 σ/√n`). Every threshold is a named constant in the module and echoed
into the JSON under `<section>.thresholds`. Per-episode / per-kick / per-hop /
per-contact records are in the JSON for re-analysis.

**Tracker source.** Every log carries two `TrackerWrapperPacket` sources:
`ER-FORCE` (200 Hz, no `kicked_ball`, ball z absent in most logs) and `TIGERs`
(70–80 Hz, capabilities `DETECT_FLYING_BALLS` + `DETECT_KICKED_BALLS`). The
`TIGERs` source is used in every log (selected automatically: the source with
kick events). It is used only for (a) `kicked_ball` events, (b) ball height
`z` for airborne gating and bounce *instants*, (c) a cross-check of robot
speeds. All kinematic quantities are computed from the raw `SSL_DetectionFrame`s.

**Clock alignment.** Tracker timestamps are on a different clock in some logs
(offset up to 6872 s at GO26). The tracker is aligned to the raw capture clock
by the median receive-time difference and then refined (±120 ms scan, 2 ms
step, parabolic interpolation) by matching tracked robot positions of moving
robots to the raw detections; the residual offsets are 0.2–0.9 ms.

**Raw tracks.** Ball detections are associated into one track per game with a
per-camera prediction gate (a flying ball projects to very different floor
points in the two cameras). All finite differences are taken between samples
*of the same camera*, which makes velocities immune to the per-camera
calibration offset (1–2 cm in the overlap band). Robot velocity/acceleration
use a local quadratic (Savitzky–Golay) fit over ±4 same-camera samples
(≈ ±55 ms); windows containing an identity-swap jump (> 8 cm or > 0.8 rad
between consecutive frames) are skipped. SG noise floor on standing robots:
σ_a = 0.10–0.19 m/s², σ_α = 1.4–2.3 rad/s².

**Advertised ball models** (from the vision geometry packets, i.e. what the
venue's ssl-vision told the teams): GO26 `acc_slide −3.46/−3.0`, `acc_roll
−0.30/−0.315`, `k_switch 0.66`, chip `0.65 / 0.95 / 0.47`; RC26 `−3.44`,
`−0.32`, `0.65`, chip `0.575 / 0.846 / 0.52`.

## 3. Ball rolling (flat)

**Episodes.** 1927 free-rolling episodes (no robot within 0.25 m, tracker
z < 0.03 m, start speed > 0.3 m/s, split on same-camera velocity jumps
> 0.45 m/s + 10 % or direction changes > 20°, ≥ 0.4 s, ≥ 20 samples,
≥ 0.3 m), 552 of them anchored to a tracker kick (kick start within 0.6 s
before the episode, kick position within 1.5 m). Each episode is fitted in the
position domain along its travel direction (with a per-camera offset column
when both cameras contribute), with 4-MAD outlier rejection and refit:

* A: `s = s0 + v0 t − a t²/2` (single deceleration)
* B: two-phase slide/roll with the switch time on a grid (Sumatra model)
* C: `dv/dt = −(a + b v)` (linear drag), `b` on a log grid

**Model comparison** (BIC per episode): B beats A in 70 % of episodes
(median ΔBIC 50), C beats A in 52 % (13.6), B beats C in 59 % (19.6).
Position RMS: A 2.59 mm, B 1.33 mm, C 1.81 mm (detection noise ≈ 1–1.5 mm).
The two-phase structure is justified: for kicks ≥ 2.5 m/s the fitted slide
deceleration is 2.1–3.8 m/s² against 0.25–0.46 for the roll phase.

## 5. Chips

293 flights (tracker z > 0.03 m; runs closer than 0.25 s merged; bounces =
local minima of tracker z below 0.06 m followed by a rise). Two independent
per-hop methods:

* **T (timing)**: flight time between bounce instants → `vz = g tf/2`,
  apex `= g tf²/8`; horizontal speed from the *grounded* raw positions at the
  two bounces (median of samples within ±20 ms). Uses only tracker timing and
  floor positions, not the tracker's velocities or its bounce model. Hops
  need a tracker apex ≥ 0.04 m consistent within ×2 with the timing apex.
* **R (raw projection fit)**: raw detections are floor projections through the
  camera (`p_obs = c_xy + (p − c_xy)(c_z − r)/(c_z − z)`, verified against a
  two-camera triangulation of a 0.54 m high ball to within 1 cm); each hop is
  fitted by Levenberg–Marquardt for `(x0, y0, vx, vy, vz, t0)` plus a
  per-camera offset, 3.5-MAD outlier rejection, accepted if 2D RMS < 12 mm
  and tf ≥ 0.1 s. Median RMS 2.8 mm (n 506 hops); R apex / T apex = 1.000 ±
  0.0005 (n 240), so the two methods agree.

## 9. What the data contradicts in `docs/design.md` §5

1. **`acc_roll = −0.30` constant.** The roll deceleration grows with speed
   (0.23 at < 0.25 m/s → 0.29 at 2 m/s → 0.37 at 3.5 m/s; `a ≈ 0.253 +
   0.019 v²` pooled, or `0.23 + 0.05 v` below 2 m/s) and differs by 28 %
   between the two carpets (−0.26 GO26, −0.33 RC26). The single number is a
   compromise; the switch ratio is not affected.
2. **`acc_slide = −3.0`.** Fast kicks slide at −3.3 to −3.4 m/s² (GO26 −3.42,
   RC26 −3.10). −3.0 is at the low end.
3. **`chip_damping_xy_first_hop = 0.75`, `other = 0.95`, `damping_z = 0.50`
   on every bounce.** Measured 0.70 / 0.87 and 0.466 on the first bounce;
   the vertical restitution of later bounces is lower (0.38), which the
   "same z damping every bounce" rule cannot express.
4. **`ball_robot_tangent = 0.0`.** Hull contacts transfer a third of the
   tangential relative velocity (k_t = 0.33 ± 0.03).
5. **`ball_kicker_normal = 0.6`.** The kicker face is much more absorbing:
   k_n = 0.81 on rebounds, and half of all face contacts end with the ball
   captured (dribbler on). The design's 0.6 makes balls bounce off the front
   of robots twice as fast as they do in reality.
6. **`shoot_radius = 0.0885` / `seat_depth = 0.008`.** The held ball centre
   is 0.096–0.104 m out for the four strongest Div A teams (0.118 for
   Immortals): the ball is seated *at* the kicker face, not 8 mm behind it.
7. **`hold_accel = 4.0`.** Balls are typically lost at 3.0 m/s² (σ 1.05),
   mostly while braking; 4.0 is the ~85th percentile of loss accelerations.
8. **`vel_angular_max = 20 rad/s`.** Observed maxima are 9–15 rad/s, p99 ≤ 6.6.
9. **`vel_absolute_max = 3.5`** is a bound, not typical: p99 speeds are
   2.5–2.9 m/s; a 3.5 m/s default makes simulated robots faster than any
   team's real fleet.
10. **Kick as an exact vector.** Real clean kicks scatter with a core σ ≈ 3°
    around the robot heading and the chip launch angle scatters with σ ≈ 6°
    around 45°; the design has no error model for either.
11. The design's statement that the first hop's horizontal damping differs
    from later hops because of spin is **confirmed** (0.70 vs 0.87), as are
    `k_switch ≈ 0.667` (0.659 ± 0.004), full cancellation of incoming
    momentum by the kicker, hull `k_n = 0.5` (0.52), wall `k_n = 0.5`
    (0.52–0.55), and the limiter-style constant acceleration (no wheel-slip
    signature in game data).

**Model-structure changes the data justifies:**

1. *Speed-dependent rolling deceleration* (`ball/trajectory.rs` Flat phase).
   Replace the constant `acc_roll` by `a(v) = acc_roll + roll_drag · v`
   with `roll_drag ≈ 0.05 s⁻¹` (per venue 0.03–0.135), which keeps a closed
   form (`v(t) = (v0 + a/b) e^{−bt} − a/b`, `s(t)` analytic, rest time
   `ln(1 + b v0/a)/b`), or `acc_roll + c v²` with `c ≈ 0.019 s/m` (closer
   over the full 0–4 m/s range, needs numeric `time_by_distance`). Evidence:
   pooled roll-phase table in §3 (69 591 samples, monotone from 0.23 to
   0.37 m/s² between 0.1 and 3.5 m/s; the linear model beats a constant in
   52 % of episodes by ΔBIC > 10). Advertise the value at ≈ 1.5 m/s
   (−0.28) in the geometry packet for clients that assume a constant.
2. *Per-venue ball presets* (`carpet = go26 | rc26`): GO26 `acc_roll −0.26,
   acc_slide −3.42, xy_first 0.73`; RC26 `−0.33, −3.10, 0.67`; everything
   else shared. The 28 % roll difference is far larger than any fit
   uncertainty.
3. *Chip bounce*: add `chip_damping_z_other_hops ≈ 0.40` (first bounce 0.46)
   next to the existing xy first/other split (§5, n 47 later bounces from
   the raw fit). Keep the spin-based first/other selection: the ball's
   post-bounce horizontal retention is genuinely higher on later hops.
4. *Kick error model* (realism knobs, default off for determinism tests):
   direction noise σ_kick_dir ≈ 2.5° (clean-kick core σ 2.9°, of which
   ~0.5–1° is vision orientation noise), chip launch angle σ ≈ 6° around the
   commanded angle, and a per-kick speed factor that cannot be identified
   from logs (commanded speed unknown) — use the `raw peak / tracker v0`
   spread (σ 0.14) as an upper bound. Straight kick speed distribution in
   games: median 3.3 m/s, p95 6.0.
5. *Kicker face contact*: with the dribbler on, half the balls that reach
   the face are captured; the simulator's dribbler force model should be
   tuned so that a ball arriving at 1–2 m/s is held (hold_accel 3 m/s² with
   `seat_depth 0` seats it within ≈ 0.1 s), and the face's own restitution
   should be 0.2 (`ball_kicker_normal 0.8`).
6. *Dribbler holding law*: a probabilistic loss (hazard rising from 0.4 %/sample
   at 1–2 m/s² braking to 6 %/sample at 5–6 m/s²) reproduces the survival
   table better than a hard `hold_accel` threshold; if a threshold is kept,
   draw it per robot from ≈ N(3.0, 1.0) m/s² (clamped ≥ 1.5). Losses are
   overwhelmingly during braking (80 %), i.e. the ball is lost forwards; the
   current force-budget model produces exactly that if the budget is 3 m/s².
7. *Robot drive*: no evidence for the wheel-slip/traction model being
   exercised in games (p99 accelerations 4–7 m/s² < µg); the firmware limiter
   with per-team `RobotLimits` is the model that matters. Recommended
   per-team presets from §7: TIGERs `3.2 / 4.2 / 3.3 m/s`, ER-Force `3.7 /
   5.5 / 3.0`, KIKS `4.2 / 5.5 / 3.4` (speed-up / brake / max speed), with
   angular `12 rad/s`, `45 rad/s²`. The step responses show real starts
   usually use 60–70 % of the limit; the simulator does not need to model
   that (it is strategy behaviour).
8. *Vision-side effects worth keeping in mind*: a kicked ball reaches full
   speed within 1–2 frames (the sim's instantaneous impulse is fine), and
   the kick direction error correlates with robot rotation at 22 ms — the
   same magnitude as the vision delay — so a simulated "kick at last
   received command" is consistent with what teams see.

## 11. Method notes and limits

* Thresholds: `rolling.thresholds`, `kicks.thresholds`, `chips.thresholds`,
  `collisions.thresholds`, `robots.thresholds`, `dribbling.thresholds` in the
  JSON (also constants at the top of each module).
* The tracker (TIGERs) kick list includes deflections; "clean" filters are
  applied where the robot's intent matters (direction error). The kick
  *speed* distribution is the game distribution of commanded speeds, not a
  kicker-hardware property; only the maxima (p95–p99) characterise hardware.
* Rolling episodes exclude the first 0.25 m after a kick; the slide phase is
  therefore only resolved for kicks ≥ 2.5 m/s, and k_switch is extrapolated
  back to the kick with the fitted slide deceleration (tracker kick time).
* Chip method T needs a detected next bounce; method R needs ≥ 6 in-flight
  raw samples and RMS < 12 mm. Later-bounce statistics come from R only.
* Robot accelerations are SG-smoothed over ≈ 110 ms; instantaneous peaks are
  underestimated by ≈ 10–20 %, percentiles are robust to this.
* Dribble "held" samples include balls in front of a robot that is about to
  pick them up; the moving-robot subset (used for `shoot_radius`) is cleaner.
* The single-camera Div B log (The Bots–TRAPS) contributes robots, kicks and
  rolling but no chips (4 flights, 0 usable bounces).
